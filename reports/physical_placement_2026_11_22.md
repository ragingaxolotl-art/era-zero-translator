# Reusable physical placement with a destination

**Put/place** now describes a physical object reaching a specified destination through an actor's caused movement. Sentences such as **Nora put the box at the gate** work with different actors, objects and destinations. Subject, object and destination descriptions retain their own attachments. Statements, commands, questions, coordinated clauses, conditions, ordered scopes and runtime 0/1/2 use the shared story composer.

The new compound `CMP:64574c1a25d72a89aafc46ef`, convention 85, reuses the exact approved caused-displacement compound and completed-arrival root. The arriving mover is the displaced object; the actor need not arrive. Placement alone does not claim support, containment, release, ownership or continued presence. See the [semantic review](../reviews/placement_vocabulary_2026_11_22.md) and [recorded migration](../migrations/placement_vocabulary_2026_11_22.yaml).

**We put the box at the gate** asks whether the user means present or past. **Will put**, **did put**, **puts**, **placed**, **putting** and commands retain their expressed time or aspect without that question. Figurative or nonphysical target/destination labels require a focused physical interpretation. Rejecting it leaves the message unresolved rather than playing that meaning.

## Remaining boundaries

This construction uses **at**. **On**, **in**, additional instruments, missing destinations, restrictive **only**, and passive/perfect forms remain unsupported; they are not discarded to make a sentence appear successful. A destination sound identifies a contextual place, not measured coordinates or a verified external location. General recorded-audio recovery is not established by these MIDI checks. The broader translator remains unfinished.
