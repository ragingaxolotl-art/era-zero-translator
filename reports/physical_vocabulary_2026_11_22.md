# Reusable physical verbs and adjectives — November 22

The translator now defines **pull, release, fall, drop, empty and full** as reusable meanings. Eight new compounds include two foundations; the 324 roots and all 107 earlier compounds are preserved exactly. The inventory is now 115 compounds and 574 English sense records. These counts are vocabulary inventory, not a measure of unrestricted English coverage.

Pull describes a force without promising movement. Release ends a physical hold without promising a fall. Drop reuses that release and the same object's fall as a causally linked event. Empty and full specialize one physical-capacity foundation; neither is a runtime state or the simple negation of the other. The [semantic review](../reviews/physical_vocabulary_2026_11_22.md) records rejected alternatives, participant constraints and approval provenance.

The sentence composer accepts inflected forms, questions, tense, uncertainty, ability, negation, necessity and ongoing aspect in its supported constructions. Empty/full can describe subjects, objects and maintained states. Ordinary physical objects proceed directly; abstract and ambiguous uses ask a meaning question. Each alternative object is checked. Commands reuse the predicate list, including “Please do not drop the box,” rather than maintaining a separate list for each new example.

## Still unfinished

This batch does not implement figurative senses, emptying/filling verbs, arbitrary physical placement, explicit contents/capacity quantities, passive or perfect constructions, or the information-operation verbs. Names, grammar and compound frames keep their previous timing. Nested compounds are reusable but can make longer music. General microphone recovery and experimental grammar chords remain outside the shorter release.

Existing songs and private definitions retain their saved notes and data. The new vocabulary is available when translating a new or revised line; old performances are not silently rewritten. The [completion plan](../docs/full-translator-next-steps.md) remains open.
