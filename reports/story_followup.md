# Fresh story and dialogue: what the music actually said

**Seven of 17 fresh inputs carried the intended meaning:** three directly and four after a question. Seven had no complete score for the intended reading. Three produced a score that changed the meaning. A playable score alone does not establish a correct translation.

| First exposure | Preserved directly | Clarified and then preserved | No complete score | Changed meaning |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Initial nine | 1 | 3 | 3 | 2 |
| Reserved eight | 2 | 1 | 4 | 1 |
| All 17 | **3** | **4** | **7** | **3** |

**Order and evidence.** [Six dictionary additions](https://github.com/ragingaxolotl-art/era-zero-translator/commit/69c89317068d1eedbbfade2ff63a47a6c3e3ecf6) came first. The [sentence and listener changes](https://github.com/ragingaxolotl-art/era-zero-translator/commit/1d91f9c927035029584bc84e41b45b3c2fec5980) came second. Then we [committed all 17 intentions](https://github.com/ragingaxolotl-art/era-zero-translator/commit/de21b29fdd7de7356c6c835e70cea40d67b9968a) before trying the lines, recorded the [first nine results](story_followup_initial.json), and only then opened the [reserve](story_followup_reserve.json). No vocabulary, grammar or scoring changes were made between these two runs.

Each row links its exact manuscript span. Narration, direct speech, individual actions, connected sentences and harder constructions were selected on purpose. This is not an estimate of the percentage of all English the system understands. None of the full inputs repeats the [earlier 29](story_readings.md); two passages reuse one previously tested clause in a new combination. Dialogue quotes omit their surrounding quotation marks; the speech-tag comma after “return” becomes a sentence-ending period.

The mobile-width browser was separately exercised on all 17 inputs with an empty personal dictionary. For scored readings we sent the MIDI bytes to the public listener with only the shared vocabulary and public definitions. Where the intention specifies an event graph, we compared independently named participants (including their phonemes and ownership), roles, predicates, time, scope and references against the ledger. Written requirements were checked against the output for the other constructions. A word sketch, a list of unrelated meanings, or a score with the wrong participant earns no success credit.

## What to work on next

1. Ask about ambiguous subjects when either an active program or its preceding output could be “it”. Give noun-like quantifiers such as “a few” a group interpretation, or decline to score them until that distinction can be played. These two changes prevent confident wrong answers.
2. Separate conveying work or information from physically carrying a load, and admitting a request from authorizing a release. Use passages from the stories to define those senses and their near misses.
3. Preserve modifying nouns and participles (a comparison request, a resumed stream), numbered locations, and maintaining a state over time. Test both/earlier experience and explicit “now” in new contexts. Keep fresh passages reserved for those changes.

The [250 implementation checks](translator_completion_regression.json) protect existing behavior; the [earlier story audit](story_readings.md) and this report measure meaning. The [acoustic tests](../tests/story_acoustic_test.mjs) recovered five clean synthesized monophonic WAVs. The 17-line manuscript evaluation used MIDI, not a speaker-to-microphone recording. Neither a general microphone decoder nor unrestricted English coverage is established.

Reproduce the diagnostic runs with `node tools/audit_story_followup.mjs initial` and `node tools/audit_story_followup.mjs reserve`. The [intentions](../tests/story_followup_intentions.json), [observed browser decisions](story_followup_browser.json) and the two raw decoder outputs are retained.
