# Ordinary time words in existing story sentences

This parser extension reuses the published previous-day, current-day, next-day and speech-time frames. It adds no roots, compounds, dictionary senses or sound frames. These words constrain the occurrence in time; runtime 0/1/2 remains independent. The day is relative to the speaker's date, not an invented absolute date or the listener's playback date.

The earlier parser already accepts some dated states and trailing-now actions. Its successful readings run first and retain their exact meaning and performance. Only a failed parse receives the new temporal-adverb pass. That pass extracts a leading word followed by a comma, or a lowercase trailing word, before recognizing the subject, command, object, destination and passive by-participant. Capitalized and quoted noun names remain names. A genuine omitted target still needs its referent; a time word cannot supply that target.

Supported new sentences use one clause per sentence, with period-separated sentences retaining the existing neutral reference rules. The time frame wraps the clause's earlier ordered tense, negation and aspect scopes. Yesterday can resolve an otherwise ambiguous present/past form such as put to past. Ordinary calendar time does not infer event completion, change a state into an action or discard a description.

New dated modal constructions ask whether the time constrains the action/state or the ability, possibility or need. The first choice places the date inside that modal; the second leaves it outside the whole claim. Other scope order remains intact, including negation outside ability in cannot. An alternative relationship remains unresolved. Multiple nested modals remain unsupported because a two-choice interface would conceal additional possible attachments. Earlier accepted modal state/now conventions are preserved; this change does not claim to have unified every existing parser's time interpretation.

New timed linked clauses and quantified subjects remain unsupported where their date attachment would require another scope decision. Repeated time phrases, morning/evening and numeric dates, perfect events, added manner/tool phrases and unsupported verbs do not lose words to obtain a result. New comparative/calendar grammar and general dialogue/date context remain outside this increment. Existing physical/information sense choices, personal meanings and before/after state guards still apply.

Validation must independently recover expected predicates, participants, references, scope order, speech act and runtime from actual generated melody and expanded chords. Frozen accepted day/state/name performances must stay exact. Whole-sentence browser checks must verify choices, exports, keyboard use, stale-result clearing and layout. These MIDI checks do not establish recorded speaker-to-microphone recovery. This implementation review follows the user's standing project instructions; it is not a separate user approval of each example.
