# Epistemic curiosity draft

The implemented reading of “curious about” is epistemic interest: resolving some
contextually relevant uncertainty about the topic is motivationally attractive in
itself. Practical motives may coexist; purely instrumental demand is insufficient.
It requires neither pleasure, conscious feeling, attention, intention, action nor
successful discovery. Knowing much about a topic does not exclude curiosity.
Oddness, sensory novelty seeking, degree modifiers and explicit whether/wh content
are not silently mapped to this reading.

The contract reuses the exact original desire foundation identity and definition.
Its desired outcome is informational resolution for the same experiencer, using
contextual inquiry content. The outcome is desired, not asserted as accomplished.
The inquiry supplies no particular question or answer. This is an explicit draft
convention specializing desire, not a claim that desire alone entails curiosity.
The standalone document fixes the desired outcome and participant binding; ordinary
desire IR still requires a proposition, and existing role validation is unchanged.

Music reuses desire's head/runtime tail, participant/content roles, inquiry/topic
frames, negation, time and speech-act contours. The ±39 convention carries the
additional epistemic-interest/outcome contract. No root or root family is added.
Twenty-seven authored full sentences recover these meanings from music, including
runtime trits, present/past, negation, questions, deictics, multiword names and
demonstrative topics. Singular they requires explicit binding. Rehashed changes
that assert the outcome, substitute attention or change its experiencer reject.
Shared draft dispatch requires one unique interpretation. Frame collision checks
cover existing canonical and draft grammar. These are implementation checks, not
representative held-out coverage or evidence of users' intended reading.

The new low frame exposed the audio detector's 30 Hz lower bound. Extending the
search to 20 Hz permits default-anchor framing notes; 112 clean low-note/duration
cases now cover MIDI 21–48. This does not establish physical speaker reproduction
at those frequencies; a practical playback register needs physical-channel testing.
One authored clean synthetic WAV now recovers a past negated curiosity question,
demonstrative topic and runtime from WAV alone through shared dispatch. It supplies
no source sentence, expected notes or hidden meaning to the decoder. This is not
physical recording evidence. The main draft UI now offers playback scheduling and complete MIDI/meaning
exports, with explicit pronoun fields and number selection for they/them. Actual
handler tests cover source/binding changes, asynchronous completion, registry-load
failures and stale exports. Ruby regeneration, live layout/listening, physical
recordings, broader grammar and canonical admission remain open.
