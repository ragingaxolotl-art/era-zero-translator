# REP:000–022 Adversarial Semantic Design Review

Status: **REVIEW EVIDENCE — CANONICAL APPROVAL RECORDED SEPARATELY**

This packet challenges all nine provisional Repair entity meanings. It compares five genuinely different architectures per address, scores 45 candidates across 360 criterion values, and routes 100 English concepts without forcing broad words into atoms.

## Packet-level design decision

The strongest architecture makes `REP:0xx` the substrate-neutral entity/condition layer for restoration. The inward triplet identifies an acceptable condition internal to one entity, the outward triplet identifies the acceptable condition of one distinct target, and the systemic triplet identifies one correlated acceptable condition across multiple participants. Persistence, present realization, and dormancy supply the exact third-trit contrast.

The third-trit grid is deliberately not a three-way exclusive partition. Each `...0` member asserts persistence of restoration-condition identity and is neutral about present realization, so it may co-occur with either its `...1` or `...2` sibling. The `...1` and `...2` siblings are mutually exclusive for the same condition at the same reference time. Runtime `[2]/num` remains uncertainty about the selected claim rather than a lexical sibling.

The design rejects the provisional mixture of restored states, active recovery processes, recoverability, damage, care, maintenance queues, backups, and future deployment. Processes remain for `REP:1xx`, affordances and enabling relations for `REP:2xx`, and domain states, records, rules, observer states, and time for their respective families.

#### restored integrity

**Invariant candidate:** An entity's inward health, function, or correctness has been returned to a stable condition.

- Fit: The provisional title follows the inward and persistent slot labels and uses familiar Repair vocabulary such as restored, recovery, care, repair, maintenance, and restore point.
- Useful coverage: It reaches common talk about health, service recovery, healed targets, maintenance queues, backups, and recovered systems.

- Conflicts and limitations: The triplets do not preserve one entity/condition center; active roots duplicate REP:1xx processes and dormant roots absorb WRN damage, DAT backups, ORI future time, and REP:2xx recoverability.

#### persistent internal restoration condition

**Invariant candidate:** An acceptable condition internal to one reference entity that remains identified as that entity's restoration standard independently of whether it is presently realized.

- Fit: The first trit stays entity/condition; the second alone selects an acceptable condition internal to one entity, assigned to one distinct target, or correlated across multiple system participants; the third alone selects persistent identity, present realization, or dormancy.
- Useful coverage: It supplies compact atoms for restored state, accepted baseline, recovered service, healed condition, target restoration, shared recovery, and comparison to a restoration reference across ordinary and technical domains.
- Territory consumed: Only the identity and present realization of a substrate-neutral restoration condition. Damage, repair processes, recoverability, resources, records, rules, observer belief, and time remain separate.
- Conflicts and limitations: The phrase acceptable condition requires an applicable restoration frame, but factoring the frame from its record or rule yields stable atoms and avoids domain-specific notions of health, correctness, or physical integrity.

#### persistent internal remedy reserve

**Invariant candidate:** A persistent restorative resource assigned to inward scope.

- Fit: Internal reserves, target remedies, and shared recovery resources are tangible Repair-family entities that can persist, engage, or remain dormant.

- Territory consumed: It consumes matter, artifacts, care roles, stored data, and deployment status rather than the restored condition those resources may help realize.
- Conflicts and limitations: MAT and CRE own physical resources, LIF owns caregivers and medicine as living or biological entities, DAT owns backups, and one resource does not guarantee restoration.

#### persistent internal maintenance state

#### persistent internal baseline record

### D. Recommended permanent semantic specification

**Title:** persistent internal restoration condition

**Invariant:** An acceptable condition internal to one reference entity that remains identified as that entity's restoration standard independently of whether it is presently realized.

**Inclusion boundary:** Includes only the persistent internal restoration condition as a substrate-neutral claim about one acceptable restoration condition internal to one reference entity. The claim is about the entity's own restoration condition, not a condition assigned to another target, an observer's judgment, or a baseline record. It is neutral about physical or biological constitution, observer knowledge, record accuracy, formal validity, damage, fault, repair method, recoverability, probability, timing, and cause.

**Excluded meanings:**

- The person, organism, material object, software component, institution, or system that bears, defines, observes, records, or realizes the restoration condition.
- Physical intactness, biological health, logical validity, data correctness, cleanliness, availability, or social reconciliation without the separate restoration-relative claim.
- Damage, fault, hazard, degradation, injury, illness, error, outage, conflict, or any other adverse condition merely because restoration might be desirable.
- Baseline record, checkpoint, backup, golden image, measurement, diagnosis, belief, criterion, policy, acceptance rule, or evidence about the condition.
- Repairing, healing, recalibrating, restoring, maintaining, replacing, rebuilding, recovering, or coordinating restoration as a process.
- Repairability, recoverability, restorative capacity, remedy resource, caregiver, service relationship, scheduled restoration, future realization, cause, or guarantee of success.

**Address justification:**

- `first_trit_0`: Selects an entity or condition: persistent internal restoration condition is an acceptable restoration condition, not a restoration process, capacity, enabling relation, resource, record, rule, or schedule.
- `second_trit`: Selects inward/self scope: one acceptable restoration condition internal to one reference entity and no other Repair-family column.
- `third_trit`: Selects the persistent/realization-neutral member by condition identity or present realization while leaving runtime truth status, observer knowledge, tense, probability, and restoration process separate.

**Nearest neighbors:** `REP:001`, `REP:002`, `REP:010`, `REP:020`, `REP:100`, `REP:200`

**Same-family boundary:** `REP:0xx` owns restoration conditions as entities; `REP:1xx` remains the layer for actual restoration processes; `REP:2xx` remains the layer for recoverability, repairability, and enabling restoration relations. Present realization in 0xx does not name the process that achieved or maintains it, and an identified restoration condition does not entail that restoration is possible. The persistent member is realization-neutral: it may co-occur with the manifest sibling or the dormant sibling at the same reference time. It asserts continuity of restoration-condition identity rather than a third mutually exclusive realization value.

**Runtime-state interpretation:**

- `[0]`: Denies that this internal restoration condition remains identified as the applicable restoration standard; it does not select the manifest or dormant sibling and does not establish a different acceptable condition.
- `[1]`: Affirms the continuing identity of this internal restoration condition without deciding whether it is presently realized, how it was established, whether it is recorded, or whether restoration is possible.
- `[2/num]`: Leaves unresolved whether the persistent internal restoration condition remains identified; the token stays the stable lexical root and does not become the manifest or dormant sibling.

**Lexical/runtime distinction:** Lexical third trit `0` permanently selects the persistent/realization-neutral member of this restoration-condition triplet. Runtime `[2]/num` instead leaves the truth or applicability of that already-selected root unresolved; it never changes realization status. Thus a dormant root affirmed with `[1]` is a confirmed identified-but-unrealized condition, while a persistent or manifest root carrying `[2]/num` remains lexically persistent or manifest but uncertain as a token claim.

**Title:** persistent shared restoration condition

**Invariant:** One correlated acceptable condition that remains identified across multiple participant entities within a shared system as their restoration standard independently of its present realization pattern.
