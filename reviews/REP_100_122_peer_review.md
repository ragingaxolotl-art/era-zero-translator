# REP:100–122 Adversarial Semantic Design Review

Status: **REVIEW EVIDENCE — CANONICAL APPROVAL RECORDED SEPARATELY**

This packet challenges all nine provisional Repair process meanings from first principles. It compares five genuinely different semantic architectures at every address, scores 45 candidates across 360 criterion values, and routes 105 English concepts without forcing broad words into atoms.

## Packet-level decision

The strongest architecture makes `REP:1xx` a substrate-neutral restorative-transformation grid. `REP:100–102` expresses restorative change originating within one reference entity. `REP:110–112` expresses restorative change caused in one target by one distinct source. `REP:120–122` expresses one coordinated transformation by which a shared restoration condition becomes realized across multiple participants. The third trit changes only completion phase.

The grid composes directly with canonical `REP:0xx`: internal, target, and shared restoration conditions provide the semantic destination while `REP:1xx` supplies transformation toward realization. It excludes concrete mechanics, generic change, maintenance, validation, records, replacement, reconstruction, coordination alone, resources, recoverability, probability, planning, and scheduling.

#### self-repair complete

**Invariant candidate:** An entity has completed an inward process of healing, correction, recalibration, or restoration.

- Fit: The provisional choice follows the inward/self and completed slot labels and favors familiar Repair language such as healing, correction, recalibration, restoration, maintenance, and recovery.
- Useful coverage: It reaches common talk about self-repair, target repair, system recovery, maintenance cycles, queued work, and staged recovery.
- Territory consumed: It mixes restorative transformation, maintenance, rebuilding, possibility, schedules, preparation, plans, resources, coordination, and stable results.
- Conflicts and limitations: The lexical 1x2 roots improperly encode possibility, queues, schedules, or plans; the systemic triplet mixes maintenance and restoration; and several definitions collide with CRE, DAT, CON, LOG, and ORI.

#### completed internal restoration

**Invariant candidate:** Completion of a restorative transformation originating within one reference entity by which that entity enters its applicable internal restoration condition.

- Fit: Every address selects one substrate-neutral transformation toward realizing an applicable restoration condition; the second trit alone distinguishes internally originating restoration, one-source target restoration, or coordinated restoration across multiple system participants, and the third alone selects phase.
- Useful coverage: It covers heal, repair, restore, recover, recalibrate, reconcile, rehabilitate, remediate, return to service, and coordinated recovery across ordinary and technical domains.
- Territory consumed: Only restorative change and its scope. Domain mechanics, movement, maintenance, replacement, records, validation, coordination, resources, probability, plans, and time remain separate.
- Conflicts and limitations: The applicable restoration condition must be resolved from REP:0xx or context, but that factoring is compositional and prevents this process atom from absorbing domain-specific health, integrity, or correctness.

#### completed internal maintenance

**Invariant candidate:** A completed upkeep process that preserves an already acceptable condition at inward/self scope.

- Fit: Internal upkeep, target servicing, and systemic maintenance naturally use inward, outward, and shared scope with completed, active, or nonactualized phases.
- Useful coverage: It covers service, preserve, sustain, clean, patch, inspect, tune, lubricate, and administer upkeep.
- Territory consumed: Preservation of an existing acceptable condition, recurring service, inspection, resource use, and organizational responsibility.
- Conflicts and limitations: Maintenance need not move anything toward restoration, restoration can occur without ongoing maintenance, and the architecture collides with LOG rules, DAT schedules, MAT actions, and CON service relations.

#### completed internal revalidation

**Invariant candidate:** A completed verification process that tests a corrected condition against a criterion at inward/self scope.

- Fit: Self-checking, target correction verification, and systemic revalidation can be framed as Repair processes at all three scopes.
- Useful coverage: It covers test, validate, verify, reconcile, audit, certify, check correction, and confirm recovery.
- Territory consumed: Rule satisfaction, evidence acquisition, comparison, records, and observer judgment rather than restoration itself.
- Conflicts and limitations: LOG owns validity and acceptance, DAT owns measurements and evidence, MIN owns judgment, and a restored condition can exist without validation.

#### completed internal reconstruction

**Invariant candidate:** A completed replacement or reconstruction process that substitutes newly made structure at inward/self scope.

- Fit: Self-rebuilding, replacement of a target, and reconstruction of a shared system are common ways to accomplish repair and support all phases.
- Useful coverage: It covers replace, rebuild, reconstruct, regenerate, refabricate, reinstall, recreate, and swap components.
- Territory consumed: Creation, destruction, substitution, identity continuity, structural assembly, and material operations.
- Conflicts and limitations: CRE and STR own those transformations, many repairs do not replace or rebuild, and a replacement can occur without restoration of the original bearer.

### Recommended permanent semantic specification

**Title:** completed internal restoration

**Invariant:** Completion of a restorative transformation originating within one reference entity by which that entity enters its applicable internal restoration condition.

**Boundary:** Includes only a completed substrate-neutral restorative transformation that originates within one reference entity and changes that entity toward its applicable internal restoration condition without requiring a distinct external source. If a distinct external source causes the change, use target restoration; if one shared condition is realized across several participants, use systemic restoration. The transformation may be realized physically, biologically, computationally, informationally, socially, or conceptually, but it does not itself assert the bearer, concrete mechanics, movement path, record, observer knowledge, validation, maintenance, replacement, resource, recoverability, probability, schedule, or causal history beyond the scope roles required by the invariant.

**Excluded meanings:**

- The restoration condition, bearer, restorative source, target, participant, material object, organism, software component, institution, checkpoint, or data record as an entity.
- Spatial movement, return, route traversal, physical force, biological mechanism, data copying, structural reconfiguration, creation, replacement, or reconstruction as such without the REP restorative transformation claim.
- Planning, intention, staging, queueing, scheduling, permission, readiness, probability, futurity, obligation, or specification—especially for lexical 1x2.
- Inspection, validation, verification, certification, evidence collection, belief revision, diagnosis, recognition, or measurement of a restoration condition.
- Maintenance, preservation, routine servicing, warning, containment, neutral coordination, fabrication, replacement, reconstruction, or mere communication about restoration.
- Recoverability, repairability, restorative capacity, remedy availability, caregiver relation, service contract, resource readiness, probability, or other continuing capacity or relation.

**Address justification:**

- `first_trit_1`: Selects a restorative process, action, or transformation rather than a restoration-condition entity, recoverability affordance, resource, record, rule, or schedule.
- `second_trit`: Selects inward/self scope: this root originates within one reference entity and changes that entity toward its applicable internal restoration condition without requiring a distinct external source; no competing family or temporal phase is added by this axis.
- `third_trit`: Selects the completed phase of the same internal-restoration process; planning, queueing, schedule, futurity, permission, probability, and runtime truth status remain separate.

**Nearest neighbors:** `REP:101`, `REP:102`, `REP:110`, `REP:120`, `REP:000`, `REP:200`

**Same-family boundary:** REP:0xx owns internal, target, and shared restoration-condition entities. REP:1xx owns restorative transformations toward realizing those conditions. REP:2xx remains reserved for recoverability, repairability, and enabling restoration relations. Internal restoration has no required distinct external source. Target restoration preserves one source and one target as distinct roles. Systemic restoration requires one coordinated transformation that realizes one shared restoration condition across multiple participants. A completed REP:1xx root entails that the applicable restoration condition was realized by the stated transformation at completion; it does not entail that the result persists, that it was recorded or validated, that maintenance continues, or that a continuing REP:2xx affordance holds unless separately asserted.

**Runtime meanings:**

- `[0]`: Denies that this internal-restoration process reached completion in the stated restoration context; it does not assert that the active or nonactualized sibling holds.
- `[1]`: Affirms that this internal-restoration process reached completion and realized the applicable restoration condition, without asserting the concrete mechanism, record, validation, permanence, maintenance, replacement, or continuing recoverability.
- `[2/num]`: Leaves unresolved whether this internal-restoration process reached completion or whether that completed result remains applicable; the lexical root stays completed.

**Lexical/runtime distinction:** The lexical third trit permanently selects the completed member of this process triplet. Runtime `[2]/num` instead qualifies the truth, applicability, or resolution of a token of that already-selected root; it never changes the root into a planned, future, or different-phase process.
