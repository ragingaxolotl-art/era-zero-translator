# Contextual thermal adjectives

The established temperature measurement and scalar comparison grammar can express a user-supplied operational meaning for hot or cold. It cannot infer one universally correct threshold from the adjective alone. The browser therefore requests a thermodynamic-temperature sense and a numeric standard, without pre-filling a number. Other readings stay unresolved.

For the chosen translation, hot means strictly greater than the supplied temperature point; cold means strictly less than it. The bound is part of semantic IR and music, not merely an explanatory note. The selection is recorded separately as provenance. Equal Celsius/Kelvin points normalize identically. Negation scopes the comparison and remains independent of runtime 0/1/2; not hot is not silently replaced with cold.

This is a contextual stipulation, not a new universal definition of the English adjectives or a new root/compound. It does not claim the speaker originally supplied a numeric threshold. The user explicitly supplies that missing interpretation before sound is produced. Physical temperature differs from felt warmth, spiciness and emotional tone. Reference-class standards, tolerance bands, vague boundaries and nonthermal senses remain further work.

Question, negation, modal, deictic and current/past calendar wrappers preserve the standard through the existing sentence composer. Future and multi-clause combinations cannot bypass the missing-standard gate; they remain incomplete. Invalid decimal/unit inputs are rejected without losing editable form values. A changed source input cannot receive the previous selection's result.
