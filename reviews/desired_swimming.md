# Desired swimming

Candidate desire now accepts “Nora wants to swim” and explicit-subject variants.
The bare infinitive denotes a bounded swimming episode in the desired content;
it does not assert actual occurrence, ability, intention, a destination or
crossing completion. “Wants to be swimming” preserves the existing activity
predicate without imposing that bounded viewpoint. “Wants not to swim” negates
the desired episode; “does not want to swim” negates the desire relation.

The existing swimming compound (conventions 21 and 20) is reused unchanged.
Controlled subjects bind only through its exact h/s-actor slot, validated in
JavaScript and Ruby. Explicit-subject desire retains its independent reference.
The shared swimming tree reader now supports the independent foundation MIDI
inverse, including the parent-desire subject marker, rather than consulting the
input sentence or formal IR to infer identity.

Twenty-two cases cover outer/inner runtime states, negation, explicit and bound
subjects, questions and progressive wording. Full formal/MIDI output matches Ruby;
independent decoding recovers water propulsion, actor binding and intensional
content. Wrong subject-slot binding is rejected. Existing standalone swimming
and across-region tests pass after sharing the inverse tree reader.

“Wants to swim across the river” remains unsupported: the desired path completion
must not be silently replaced with ongoing traversal. Candidate desire remains
opt-in and no canonical root, compound, address or performance was changed.
