# Sensory registration: first reusable sensory foundation

Status: approved under the user's delegated translator-completion goal, October
28, 2026. Compound `CMP:ade73037114406a2cd91ce13`, definition 1.0.0, reserves
`EZLEX-1` code **0** for `ongoing_sensory_registration`. This is an explicit lexical
extension; no existing root or compound changes meaning.

The invariant is an ongoing source-to-target information transfer physically
realized by material coupling, where sensory transduction causes the receiver to
register information carried by the physical source stimulus. The source and
receiver are distinct. A biological receiver and a device may both instantiate
this registration sense. Consciousness is not required or denied.

## Why these ingredients fit

DAT:111 defines an ongoing information transfer from one source context to a
distinct target, causing that target to receive the information. MAT:211 defines
active physical influence from a material bearer to a distinct target. The
qualifier restricts the physical realization of the same transfer; it does not
assert an unrelated coupling elsewhere. The conventional addition specifies
sensory transduction. It contributes something neither root nor their unmarked
composition entails, and its code is audible around the whole compound.

Registration is not all physical influence: warming an unresponsive rock is a
counterexample. It is not all information transfer: copying a stored file is a
counterexample. A device can register a stimulus without any warranted claim of
experience. A person can register input without attending to or recognizing it.
The definition does not infer a channel, a recognized object, communicative
intent, truth of an interpretation, or understanding.

This first sense is restricted to a distinct external physical source. It does
not cover internally generated sensation, hallucination, introspection, stored
memory, or the broader philosophical notion of any possible sensory experience.
Those require their own definitions, not relaxed use of this one.

## Participants and ordinary construction

“The sensor is registering sensory input from the bell” supplies:

- **target:** the receiver, “the sensor”;
- **source:** the physical stimulus source, “the bell”;
- **predicate:** ongoing sensory registration.

The nouns remain phonemic references; “bell” does not automatically mean an
auditory channel. A bell might be observed by a visual sensor. The construction
asserts registration from that source but leaves the specific sensory modality
unspecified. A later auditory or visual compound must state its channel instead
of guessing it from a noun.

The phrase identifies the ongoing process. Runtime **0**, **1**, and **2/num**
remain separate occurrence endings; none changes its lexical address or turns
registration into attention, completed perception, or a future intention.
Negation and modality remain outside the predicate, with source/receiver roles
preserved under their scopes.

## Dependency order and remaining work

Channel-specific registration can reuse this exact version. Conscious sensory
experience and successful perceptual access are separate foundations: neither
may be obtained by merely renaming registration. Seeing and hearing should reuse
only the foundations their eventual definitions entail. Looking and listening
may then add attention where intended. Recognition and understanding require
additional success criteria.

The score record is an implementation-author review, not independent linguistic
validation. Tests establish source/receiver preservation, scope, runtime endings,
and music contrasts; they do not prove arbitrary English coverage or perceptual
recognition by listeners. The release remains a draft.
