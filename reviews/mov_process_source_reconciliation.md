# Movement examples and source provenance

The carrying milestone requires distinguishing approach, completed arrival and
potential movement. The applied `migrations/MOV_100_122_semantic_approval.yaml`
already establishes those invariant definitions and explicitly excludes intention,
planning, queuing, readiness and future time from lexical 1x2. It distinguishes
lexical 1x2 from runtime state 2. No additional root is needed for these contrasts.

However, copied explanatory prose still described MOV:102 as prepared departure,
MOV:112 as intended or queued movement, and MOV:120–122 as route-wide or temporal
transitions. Eighteen neighbor-note strings also retained provisional labels such
as departure readiness, pending arrival and system transition.

The editorial migration corrects five examples and eighteen neighbor-note strings
on the nine MOV process roots. It updates the four active inventories/specification
copies and the browser index. The compact `all_atoms` copy and its browser index
have no neighbor notes; only their existing examples change. All invariant definitions, titles, exclusions,
addresses, lexical trits, runtime examples and sound fields remain unchanged.
Historical proposals and their generators remain unchanged as source evidence.

The migration records every before/after field and whole-inventory fingerprint.
Validation reverses only those recorded prose changes, then the earlier MIN
editorial correction, to prove compatibility with the original historical pins.
Unrecorded definition, sound, runtime or prose mutations fail that check. This is
not permission to treat any future editorial change as semantically harmless.

The repository citation audit separately examined every `reviews/` path in current
root review-history records: 288 roots cite 198 unique paths, of which 47 are
missing at their cited locations. Thirty-six roots have no such path field.
These are repository metadata findings, not a claim that the sources do not exist
in Google Drive, ChatGPT or another archive, nor proof that those roots lack approval.

For MOV specifically, the cited `MOV_100_122_approval_record.md` and
`MOV_100_122_final_challenge.md` are absent, while the applied selective migration
is present. The older peer review still declares its recommendation noncanonical;
the migration overrides it for the nine approved process invariants. The cited
MOV:200–222 validation file is also absent. Its available peer review explains
why availability must not imply movement, but it is not itself an approval record.

Targeted Google Drive searches for both MOV block filenames returned the integrated
[DBA ERA 0 Language document](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1btnSrT37YJt1aD4gXGtJBO8e3ZRG6COYmFLaRkykHA8/edit).
Its “USER APPROVAL — MOV:100–122 SEMANTIC CANONIZATION” section independently
corroborates the process approval and 1x2/runtime separation. Its later “SEMANTIC
AUTHORITY UPDATE — 81 CANONICAL ROOTS” explicitly records delegated approval of
MOV:200–222. Thus the earlier pending-review banner is historical, not the final
status of that block. The [retrieval evidence](../reports/mov_google_authority_retrieval.json)
records the document's October 10 modification time, a hash of retrieved readable
text, and the exact relevant passages. This corroboration does not recover the
missing standalone challenge/validation files or complete the original turn ledger.

pursuit, routes, schedules and temporal systems. They are recorded for a separate
authority reconciliation; this process-layer migration does not silently rewrite
