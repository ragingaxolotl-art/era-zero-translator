# ORI:200–222 Adversarial Semantic Design Review

Status: **REVIEW EVIDENCE — CANONICAL APPROVAL RECORDED SEPARATELY**

This packet challenges all nine provisional Origin relation meanings. It compares five genuinely different architectures per address, scores 45 candidates across 360 criterion values, and routes 115 English concepts without forcing broad words into atoms.

## Packet-level design decision

The strongest architecture makes `ORI:2xx` the substrate-neutral ontological-relation layer paired with canonical `ORI:0xx` conditions and `ORI:1xx` transitions. The inward triplet owns source-grounding capacity, the outward triplet owns target-ground dependence, and the systemic triplet owns shared-world constraint. Persistent, operative, and latent supply the exact third-trit contrast.

Lexical latency means the relation belongs without present operation; it does not mean uncertain, possible, intended, scheduled, future, or unknown. Runtime `[2]/num` remains uncertainty about the selected status claim. The design rejects the provisional mixture of possibility, causation, contingency, universality, and possible worlds.

### D. Recommended permanent semantic specification

**Title:** persistent source-grounding capacity

**Invariant:** A persistent ontological capacity of one source to ground a specified state of affairs, independently of whether that capacity is presently operative.

**Inclusion boundary:** Includes only a persistent source-grounding capacity as a substrate-neutral relation in what may obtain within a specified reality. It is neutral about physical mechanism, temporal order, probability, intention, observation, representation, logical proof, software implementation, social permission, productive agency, structural arrangement, danger, and repair. It neither creates its relata nor asserts that a formal description is true.

**Excluded meanings:**

- The source-ground, distinct-existence, or shared-world condition itself.
- A bounded transition that establishes, actualizes, or changes an ontological condition.
- Causal force, productive method, temporal priority, provenance history, physical disposition, biological capacity, mental possibility, permission, or plan.
- Formal entailment, satisfiability, probability, counterfactual accessibility, data dependency, structural dependency, or software constraint without explicit ontological scope.
- Observation, evidence, representation, truth, validity, certainty, consensus, authorship, ownership, success, or failure.
- Any equation of lexical latency with runtime uncertainty, futurity, intention, scheduling, or mere ignorance.

**Address justification:**

- `first_trit_2`: Selects an ontological relation, capacity, dependence, or constraint rather than an entity condition or bounded transition.
- `second_trit`: Selects inward/source orientation and therefore source-grounding capacity, not another Origin-family column.
- `third_trit`: Selects persistent lexical status while leaving token truth, observer knowledge, tense, probability, causation, and physical mechanism separate.

**Nearest neighbors:** `ORI:201`, `ORI:202`, `ORI:210`, `ORI:220`, `ORI:000`, `ORI:100`

**Same-family boundary:** `ORI:0xx` owns ontological conditions: source-ground, distinct existence, and shared-world condition. `ORI:1xx` owns bounded transitions that establish or change those conditions. `ORI:2xx` owns ontological relations and capacities: source-grounding capacity, target-ground dependence, and shared-world constraint. A relation does not entail that either relatum presently exists, that a transition is occurring, or that a causal mechanism has acted. Persistent status may coexist with either operative or latent status across compatible reference spans; operative and latent are mutually exclusive for the same relation at one reference time. Runtime `[2]/num` separately marks uncertainty about whichever lexical status was selected.

**Runtime-state interpretation:**

- `[0]`: Denies persistence of this source-grounding capacity; it does not assert absence, impossibility, reversal, or the operative or latent sibling.
- `[1]`: Affirms that this source-grounding capacity persists across the relevant reference span without asserting that it is presently operative.
- `[2/num]`: Leaves persistence of this source-grounding capacity unresolved; runtime uncertainty does not change the lexical root into operative or latent status.

**Lexical/runtime distinction:** Lexical third trit `0` permanently selects persistent status for this source-grounding capacity. Runtime `[2]/num` instead leaves that already-selected status claim unresolved. Thus a latent root with `[1]` positively asserts latent membership, while any persistent or operative root with `[2]/num` remains lexically persistent or operative but uncertain as a token claim.
