# Destination-directed carrying and its culmination

This author review implements the dependency order in the arrival and receipt
reviews. It reuses the exact support and carrying definitions before specializing
them; it adds no root or family. The added same-event relations are explicit
lexical conventions, not claimed entailments of a bare conjunction of roots.

Convention 5 specializes ongoing carrying with MOV:111. The moving load in the
approach is the very load borne by the nested carrier, and the carrying conveyance
constitutes the approach during that event. A destination slot specifies the
target position, potentially through a referent's contextual location. No arrival,
intention or recipient is inferred. The entire source need not translate, so a
conveyor remains possible.

Convention 6 depends on convention 5 and MOV:110. It adds culmination: that same
carrying event ends in that same load reaching that same destination. The nested
ongoing processes describe internal phases of the completed event; they are not
asserted to continue at speech time. This excludes carrying partway followed by
throwing or independent travel, unrelated arrivals and endpoint changes.

The outer learned convention audibly supplies the event relation. Slot constraints
independently require exact load identity and, for completed carrying, identical
destination arguments across the nested approach and arrival. The inherited
carrier/load distinction remains enforced. Equal labels on different argument
nodes are not silently treated as shared identity.

Runtime 0/1/2 belongs to the outer compound occurrence. Root lexical trits and
nested definition versions remain unchanged; neither past tense nor completed
arrival is encoded by an inactive or dormant ending. Negation, question, time and
aspect scope surround the compound rather than altering these definitions.

Existing carrying and “toward” constructions retain their definitions and sounds.
The new destination-directed definitions are separately versioned. A progressive
reading uses convention 5 and must not contain the completed-arrival root.
A completed reading uses convention 6. Habitual endpoint-carrying requires a
habitual operator around the completed event type; it does not assert one current
completed event. English parsing and clarification must select the applicable
reading without dropping a noun property or inventing receipt.

Ordinary “to” now requires a spatial interpretation, with any needed viewpoint
and mass-standard choices in one form. Progressive and habitual readings have
separate scopes as described above. Remaining work includes commands with “to”,
per-clause choices, receipt/custody and delivery, and general independent semantic
recovery. Different
sounds or hashes alone do not prove semantic adequacy. Tests must inspect nested
conventions, load/destination roles, runtime and the rejection of mismatched
arguments; broad completion gates remain open.

The controlled role adapter now accepts a repeated role only when explicit
(transitive) same-argument constraints join every slot carrying that role. It
still rejects independent repeated roles and different target/actor arguments.
The dictionary probe uses this same unambiguity check, without inferring reference
identity from labels. Six local Ruby controlled-syntax cases cover both new compounds and 0/1/2.
The hosted advanced-input box still supports only its documented simple subset;
the browser tests here use the ordinary-English composition path.

A local browser form check verified blank choices, rejection of a missing
destination choice, and successful habitual/spatial/20-kilogram translation of
“Pip carries the heavy box to Ada [2]”. The result displayed `MOV:110 [2] num: 71`
and the semantic explanation. This is form/render verification, not a listening
test or independent full-meaning decode.
