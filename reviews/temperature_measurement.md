# Thermodynamic temperature before thermal adjectives

This extends the existing measurement grammar with kelvin, appended as unit code 14. Codes 1–13 retain their meanings and sounds. MAT:010 (or MAT:000 for the speaker's own body) supplies the material bearer, and the measurement unit supplies the temperature dimension. No new atom or compound is required; no material-energy root is relabeled as hot.

The BIPM defines kelvin as the unit of thermodynamic temperature: https://www.bipm.org/en/si-base-units/kelvin . Its temperature-realization appendix specifies Celsius temperature as the difference from 273.15 K: https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/237262613/SI-App2-kelvin-2024-TG-Dig-revision/1444eda0-9ec1-7917-9eb7-41bc0d73e23a . These are scale definitions, not assumptions that every object has a uniform temperature.

“The radio has a temperature of 20 degrees Celsius” and “The radio has a temperature of 293.15 kelvins” assert the same temperature point. Exact decimal arithmetic must preserve this equivalence beyond floating-point precision. Scalar comparison bounds receive the same positive-slope affine conversion. Temperature differences do not receive the offset and are excluded from this construction.

Signed magnitudes follow the existing measurement grammar; numerical acceptance is not a physical-feasibility test. The statement attributes the supplied thermodynamic temperature to the identified material bearer in context. It does not infer spatial uniformity, a measuring instrument, precision, heat content or felt warmth. Nonuniform temperature fields require further structure.

Runtime 0/1/2 remains on the lexical occurrence and is not temperature magnitude or tense. Entity comparison, hot/cold thresholds, felt temperature, Fahrenheit, temperature changes and intervals remain separate work. A new unit is an explicit dimension convention, not proof of general adjective coverage.
