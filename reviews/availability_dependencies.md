# Availability dependencies

Status: dependency audit, not implemented vocabulary or canonical admission.
The original object-desire outcome “The tea is available” remains unsupported.
The audit report is `reports/availability_dependency_audit.json`, generated by
`tools/audit_availability_dependencies.mjs` from the current 324-root index.

## Why the existing available roots are not synonyms

MOV:201 is a mover's available capacity to change its own position; MAT:201 is
available physical work capacity; MIN:201 is attentional capacity; CON:201 is
communicative participation capacity; STR:201 is internal reconfiguration capacity.
Their 202 counterparts distinguish latent capacity. Neither member expresses
whether a particular tea portion can be obtained by a particular participant.
CON:100/120 concern expression/distribution of communicative content, not material
stock or access. Reusing their English adjective would erase the root domain.

Runtime 0/1/2 must remain separate from this lexical distinction. Runtime 1 on
“tea” cannot supply an omitted prospective user, activity, access conditions or
time interval. Runtime 2 is not a substitute for the intended modality or for an
unspecified participant. All three states must survive any eventual composition.

## Distinct readings and dependency order

1. Resource opportunity: identify a resource, prospective user, activity and
   contextual conditions. Tea being obtainable by Nora does not establish that
   Nora already owns, holds or drinks it. Availability to Eli need not imply
   availability to Nora. Start here for explicit object-desire clarification.
2. Offer/stock: being offered for sale supplies a particular acquisition context;
   it is not a completed acquisition, unrestricted access or ownership transfer.
   Reuse opportunity composition only after offer conditions are represented.
3. Functional readiness: a usable tool and an accessible tool are distinct. An
   accessible broken tool can fail the intended activity. Reuse the actual
   activity's capacity requirements rather than inferring general readiness.
4. Scheduling: a participant's availability needs a time interval and activity or
   commitment context. Free time does not entail willingness, and availability
   for a call does not imply availability for travel.

Before selecting a compound, evaluate the existing possibility scopes around an
explicit activity proposition. They already distinguish possibility from actual
occurrence, but do not automatically identify the relevant access conditions.
A new opportunity convention, if needed, must document this additional invariant
and make all meaning-bearing arguments audible. No root family is justified yet.

## Acceptance obligations

Distinguish available-to-Nora from available-to-Eli, available-for-purchase from
already-purchased, functional readiness from mere physical access, and permission
from practical opportunity. Preserve negation and question scope without asserting
that an opportunity is exercised. For “Nora wants tea,” require an explicit
availability outcome; keep that outcome intensional. Do not guess a user, purpose
or schedule from the noun's sound label.

An implementation must first model selected opportunity and conditions, then
validate English composition, music and independent decoding. Test counterexamples
where changing only participant, activity, condition or time changes meaning,
including runtime 0/1/2. Blind waveform recovery and physical recordings remain
required. This review is not a claim that an availability translation now works.
