# Availability dependencies

Status: dependency audit, not implemented vocabulary or canonical admission.
The original object-desire outcome “The tea is available” remains unsupported.
The audit report is `reports/availability_dependency_audit.json`, generated by
`tools/audit_availability_dependencies.mjs` from the current 324-root index.

## Why the existing available roots are not synonyms

MOV:201 is a mover's available capacity to change its own position; MAT:201 is
available physical work capacity; MIN:201 is attentional capacity; CON:201 is
communicative participation capacity; STR:201 is internal reconfiguration capacity.
Their 202 counterparts distinguish latent capacity. Neither member expresses
whether a particular tea portion can be obtained by a particular participant.
CON:100/120 concern expression/distribution of communicative content, not material
stock or access. Reusing their English adjective would erase the root domain.

Runtime 0/1/2 must remain separate from this lexical distinction. Runtime 1 on
“tea” cannot supply an omitted prospective user, activity, access conditions or
time interval. Runtime 2 is not a substitute for the intended modality or for an
unspecified participant. All three states must survive any eventual composition.

## Distinct readings and dependency order

1. Resource opportunity: identify a resource, prospective user, activity and
   contextual conditions. Tea being obtainable by Nora does not establish that
   Nora already owns, holds or drinks it. Availability to Eli need not imply
   availability to Nora. Start here for explicit object-desire clarification.
2. Offer/stock: being offered for sale supplies a particular acquisition context;
   it is not a completed acquisition, unrestricted access or ownership transfer.
   Reuse opportunity composition only after offer conditions are represented.
3. Functional readiness: a usable tool and an accessible tool are distinct. An
   accessible broken tool can fail the intended activity. Reuse the actual
   activity's capacity requirements rather than inferring general readiness.
4. Scheduling: a participant's availability needs a time interval and activity or
   commitment context. Free time does not entail willingness, and availability
   for a call does not imply availability for travel.

Before selecting a compound, evaluate the existing possibility scopes around an
explicit activity proposition. They already distinguish possibility from actual
occurrence, but do not automatically identify the relevant access conditions.
A new opportunity convention, if needed, must document this additional invariant
and make all meaning-bearing arguments audible. No root family is justified yet.

## Acceptance obligations

Distinguish available-to-Nora from available-to-Eli, available-for-purchase from
already-purchased, functional readiness from mere physical access, and permission
from practical opportunity. Preserve negation and question scope without asserting
that an opportunity is exercised. For “Nora wants tea,” require an explicit
availability outcome; keep that outcome intensional. Do not guess a user, purpose
or schedule from the noun's sound label.

An implementation must first model selected opportunity and conditions, then
validate English composition, music and independent decoding. Test counterexamples
where changing only participant, activity, condition or time changes meaning,
including runtime 0/1/2. Blind waveform recovery and physical recordings remain
required. This review is not a claim that an availability translation now works.

## Executed composition audit

`tools/audit_availability_composition.mjs` exercises nine full existing
compositions: possibility around past custody acquisition, negation outside that
possibility, and negation inside it, each at runtime 0/1/2. The performed MIDI
inverse recovers the exact modal/time/negation order, participants and acquisition
meaning without inferring ownership. See `reports/availability_composition_audit.json`.

Four prospective probes remain unparsed: “Nora can acquire physical custody of
the tea,” “It is possible that Nora acquires physical custody of the tea,” “The
tea is available to Nora,” and “The tea is available for Nora to drink.” The
working past-acquisition form must not be used as their substitute. Reusing its
root/compound phase contract is possible, but a new occurrence must represent a
prospective or contextual event without the past-time wrapper.

Next implementation order is therefore: separate event viewpoint from speech
time in acquisition wording; represent the explicit practical opportunity context
and selected activity; then connect the availability interpretation. Keep generic
possibility, capability and permission distinct. The nine passing probes are
reusable-component evidence, not nine successful availability translations. No
availability compound or new musical frame is introduced by this audit.

## Draft practical custody opportunity model

`pages/custody_opportunity.mjs` defines one selected availability-related sense:
contextual circumstances admit a prospective holder's acquisition of practical
physical custody of the same resource. It pins the existing acquisition compound
and its conditional before/culmination commitments. Those phases apply if the
activity is realized; the opportunity does not assert a current, ongoing or
completed acquisition. Activity time is prospective relative to the opportunity,
not automatically future relative to speech. Permission, ownership, willingness,
and availability for other activities are excluded from entailment.

The three explicit references are prospective holder, resource and access
context. A context label identifies the selected circumstances but does not
encode their rules. Same known holder/resource identity rejects, preserving the
underlying custody invariant. Unknown real-world identity is not inferred.

Forty-eight authored semantic cases distinguish holders, contexts, negation,
questions and runtime 0/1/2. Rehashed attempts to assert realization, permission,
past activity, wrong dependency, or inverted culmination reject. This is a draft
semantic contract only. Its English interpretation, audible convention,
independent inverse and recordings remain unimplemented. Explicit propositions
for the access conditions, different activities, scheduling and stock/offer
readings are still separate dependencies. No availability translation is counted
complete, no root is added, and no canonical compound is promoted here.


## Explicit availability English selection

`pages/availability_sentences.mjs` composes a selected practical-custody-opportunity
reading from singular “is/was (not) available (to ...)” statements and questions,
including negative contractions. The access context and interpretation are
required; an omitted prospective holder is an explicit additional selection.
A supplied holder cannot override one already stated. Shared neutral pronoun
resolution preserves separate resource and prospective-holder references.

Eighteen full-document comparisons check past/present, negation/question and all
runtime states. Bare availability with a selected holder matches its explicit
to-phrase, and explicitly bound it/them matches the named reading. Missing
interpretation/context/holder and conflicting holder selection reject. Sale,
scheduling and drinking-purpose wording remain unparsed. This is semantic API
composition only: no opportunity music, inverse, waveform or UI exists yet.
No default availability sense or root-family expansion is introduced.

## Independent opportunity renderer and semantic corruption checks

The Ruby opportunity renderer independently pins the opportunity contract,
acquisition dependency and conditional phase commitments. It validates all three
references, allowed scope, runtime, explicit context and distinct known
holder/resource identity before rendering. Eighteen existing authored cases match
JavaScript MIDI byte for byte.

Rehashed changes asserting permission or actual acquisition, moving activity into
past time, replacing the event dependency, invalidating runtime or collapsing
holder/resource identity reject. Two structurally valid musical corruptions—an
absent access-context role and duplicate prospective-holder role in place of the
resource—fail both the specific inverse and shared semantic dispatch. These
checks test missing meanings rather than treating different MIDI hashes as proof.
UI integration, desired availability, explicit condition propositions, broader
availability senses and physical recordings remain unfinished. The last full
regression predates this extension; this evidence is targeted.
