# Practical physical custody before transfer

Status: delegated author semantic review under the translator-completion goal.
This adds a conventional compound, not a root or an inferred synonym. Scores in
the candidate registry are author judgments, not independent validation.

## Meaning and reusable parts

Practical physical custody is established effective control over access to and
physical handling of a distinct material object, directly or through a controlled
physical intermediary. It is a relation, not a completed transfer event. Control
must already be established; being strong enough to seize a nearby object does
not qualify. A controlled enclosure may mediate custody without the holder
continuously touching, moving or supporting the object. The control need not be
exclusive: two holders can each participate in practical control. The compound
does not assert that each holder can act independently of the other.

MAT:210 supplies the established material relation that permits physical influence
from the holder to the distinct target. MAT:010 restricts the target to a material
object. Explicit convention 7 adds effective control over access and handling;
the roots do not entail that commitment by themselves. This is a lexical addition
within the existing conventional-compound architecture, not an expansion of the
root meanings. The target qualification must remain inside its role frame.

Source and target slots represent holder and held object and must bind distinct
argument occurrences. Known same-referent aliases must also fail. Unknown external
identity cannot be inferred from name phonemes, and spelling equality alone is not
a general identity model. The convention's opening/closing frame identifies this
meaning separately from support (3) and carrying (4–6).

## Boundaries and adversarial examples

| Situation | Custody commitment |
|---|---|
| A custodian controls access to a box in a controlled locker | Can satisfy custody without current touch or load bearing |
| A table supports a visitor's box | Support alone does not establish custody |
| A fan blows on the box | Physical influence alone does not establish custody |
| Ada owns a box inaccessible to her in another person's control | Ownership alone does not establish custody |
| Ada controls a borrowed box | Custody does not assert ownership or entitlement |
| A box arrives beside Ada while Pip retains control | Arrival alone does not establish Ada's custody |
| Ada could overpower the holder and take the box | Potential takeover is not established control |
| Two custodians share control of access and handling | Exclusivity is not required |
| A legal guardian has duties concerning a child | Guardianship is outside this material-object sense |
| A user has permission to read a file | Permission and information access are separate meanings |

The compound states neither lawful authority, willing acceptance, consciousness,
benefit, acquisition history nor responsibility. Institutional/legal custody,
abstract possession, taking, keeping, receiving and handing over are not automatic
aliases. An explicit “has physical custody of” sentence selects this practical
sense. Unqualified “has”, “holds”, “owns” and “receives” remain ambiguous or separate.

## 0/1/2 and dependency order

The lexical MAT:210 address stays established coupling. Runtime 0/1/2 applies
independently to this custody occurrence and to any lexical participants. Runtime
0 must not encode a relinquishment event, 1 must not imply current physical action,
and 2 must not encode a promised or future handover. Grammatical negation remains
separate from state 0. No new family or root is introduced.

A transfer must later relate before/after custody of the same object under one
event. A pair of custody statements alone does not establish transfer, causation,
loss of the former holder's control or acquisition by the new one. Delivery must
then distinguish physical arrival from that transfer. Do not alias carrying-to a
person to delivery or mark the original carrying/receipt gate complete here.
