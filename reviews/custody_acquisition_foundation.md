# Acquisition of physical custody

Status: delegated author review under the translator-completion goal. Candidate
scores are author judgments, not independent validation. No root is added or

## Root fit and conventional commitment

The applied ORI:110 approval and its detailed peer review define completed distinct
actualization as a substrate-neutral transition in which one distinct referent
comes to obtain. Its inclusion boundary is neutral about substance and mechanism.
Here the referent is the specifically bound custody relation, not the held object.
Custody already relates an exact holder to a distinct material object. Convention
8 explicitly binds that relation to the actualization: it does not obtain just
before the transition and obtains at culmination. This identification and the
pre/post linkage are additional lexical commitments, not inferred from an unmarked
qualifier graph. ORI:110's rejected causal-production example is not authority.

The nested custody statement supplies the culmination state inside the event.
It is not independently asserted at speech time. The same object need not move
or begin to exist; the same holder need not become its owner. Actualization does
not supply a causal mechanism, a former custodian, permission, intention,
willingness, or continued custody after completion. A holder can acquire control
of an unattended object or a controlled enclosure without a handover.

## Contrasts and dependency order

- Having custody throughout an interval does not establish acquisition within it.
- A box arriving beside Ada while Pip retains control does not establish Ada's acquisition.
- Ada gains practical control over a borrowed object without becoming its owner.
- Gaining legal permission alone does not establish effective practical control.
- Acquisition can occur without a prior custodian; sourced transfer needs further meaning.
- Another holder may retain shared control; this event does not assert relinquishment.
- Losing custody later does not undo the earlier acquisition.

The lexical event's completion is separate from past tense and runtime 0/1/2.
Runtime 0 does not mean relinquishment; 2 does not mean intended or future acquisition.
Negation of the completed event does not assert an attempted or ongoing acquisition.
Commands request an event; they do not assert that it happened.

Build sourced custody transfer only after this event has verified participant,
state and musical contrasts. Transfer must identify a prior holder and link the
same object's acquisition to that source. Delivery then needs its own relation
between carrying/arrival and acquisition. All original completion gates stay open.

## Implementation and evidence

The new compound is `CMP:c332d2e89b9f6177486fc93b`, version 1.0.0, convention
8. It reuses custody `CMP:0aca609416668978e1527dac` version 1.0.0, including its
nested material restriction and distinct-participant constraint. All forty prior
compound records are unchanged. The root inventory remains 324.

The sentence construction supports completed past acquisition, negative past,
past questions and commands. Commands remove past-time scope and request the
transition without asserting its occurrence. Heavy/light object restrictions use
the existing explicit mass-standard choice. Unqualified acquisition, ownership,
ongoing acquisition and source-holder phrases remain outside this construction.

`tests/custody_acquisition_test.mjs` compares 36 sentence/state/role/scope/property
cases against the Ruby renderer and exact MIDI, plus three controlled performances
with distinct holder/object runtime states. `decodeCustodyAcquisitionMidi` reads
MIDI and the root inventory only. It recovers the event, ordered scope, references,
properties and runtime, and rejects altered actualization/coupling roots and
conventions even when their musical structure remains balanced. Existing custody
music cannot be mistaken for acquisition, or vice versa.

This validates a bounded acquisition event. It does not establish general discourse,
recorded-audio recognition, a handover source, or completed delivery.
