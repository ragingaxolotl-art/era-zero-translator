# Contextual mass adjectives

Reuse the established mass measurement and scalar comparison grammar before adding a root or compound for heavy/light. The English adjectives have multiple readings: physical mass, force, effort, seriousness and (for light) brightness are not interchangeable. The implemented option explicitly selects mass and a user-supplied threshold.

For this selected translation, heavy means strictly greater than the threshold; light means strictly less. A negative adjective negates that comparison, not the runtime ending and not the opposite adjective. The threshold is encoded in the semantic IR and sound. The selected dimension and normalized threshold are also retained as interpretation provenance.

Gram/kilogram conversion uses the same exact decimal normalization as existing mass statements. Nonnegative thresholds are accepted by this construction; no number is supplied by default. Numeric user input defines the contextual standard rather than claiming a universal English cutoff. Reference-class inference, vague boundaries, burden, weight force, optical light and metaphor remain separate work.

The shared adjective-standard UI captures the chosen value, rejects replaced panels or changed source/standard, and leaves invalid entries editable. Current/past calendar, question, negation and modal wrappers retain the choice. Future and multi-clause combinations cannot silently bypass missing interpretation.
