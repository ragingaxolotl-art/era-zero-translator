# ORI:100–122 Adversarial Semantic Design Review

Status: **REVIEW EVIDENCE — CANONICAL APPROVAL RECORDED SEPARATELY**

This packet challenges all nine provisional Origin process meanings. It compares five genuinely different architectures per address, scores 45 candidates across 360 criterion values, and routes 114 English concepts without forcing broad words into atoms.

## Packet-level design decision

The strongest architecture makes `ORI:1xx` the substrate-neutral ontological-transition layer paired with canonical `ORI:0xx`. The inward triplet establishes source-ground status, the outward triplet actualizes one distinct referent, and the systemic triplet changes what obtains across one shared reality under one jointly applicable world condition. Completed, active, and nonactualized supply the exact third-trit contrast.

Lexical nonactualization means the typed transition is not occurring, not that it is possible, intended, prepared, scheduled, queued, probable, or future. Runtime `[2]/num` remains uncertainty about the selected phase claim. The design rejects the provisional mixture of general continuation, differentiation, causal production, possibility, preparedness, staging, and stable result states. Conditions remain `ORI:0xx`; causal, modal, temporal, and provenance relations remain `ORI:2xx` candidates for later review; physical change, cognition, representation, formal evaluation, and production remain separate or compounded.

### D. Recommended permanent semantic specification

**Title:** completed source-ground establishment

**Invariant:** Completion of a bounded ontological transition by which one referent becomes grounded as a source from which another state of affairs may originate.

**Inclusion boundary:** Includes only the completed source-ground establishment as a substrate-neutral ontological transition at inward scope. The process changes source-ground status only. It does not assert a causal mechanism, creator, temporal schedule, physical transformation, or produced artifact. It is neutral about substance, location, cognition, evidence, representation, logical validity, agency, authority, productive method, hazard, repair, scheduling, duration, probability, and causal mechanism.

**Excluded meanings:**

- The source-ground, distinct-existence, or shared-world condition as an entity-layer state rather than its transition.
- A causal link, physical transformation, productive act, living process, mental change, data operation, structural reconfiguration, warning, or repair process.
- Observation, detectability, evidence, truth, validity, certainty, consensus, permission, authority, success, or result persistence.
- Unrestricted possibility, necessity, probability, counterfactuality, intention, plan, readiness, schedule, queue, or future time.
- The content of a natural law, algorithm, causal mechanism, physical mechanism, social convention, or formal model.
- Any claim that completion entails persistence, that activity entails observation, or that nonactualization entails possibility, preparation, or futurity.

**Address justification:**

- `first_trit_1`: Selects a bounded ontological transition rather than an entity condition, causal or modal relation, observation, representation, or physical transformation.
- `second_trit`: Selects inward/self scope: one referent undergoing establishment as a source-ground for another state of affairs and no other Origin-family column.
- `third_trit`: Selects the completed phase of the same ontological transition while leaving runtime assertion status, observer knowledge, clock time, probability, intention, scheduling, causation, and physical mechanism separate.

**Nearest neighbors:** `ORI:101`, `ORI:102`, `ORI:110`, `ORI:120`, `ORI:000`, `ORI:100`

**Same-family boundary:** `ORI:0xx` owns source-ground, distinct-existence, and shared-world conditions. `ORI:1xx` owns the corresponding bounded ontological transitions: source-ground establishment, distinct actualization, and shared-world transition. `ORI:2xx` remains reserved for causal, modal, temporal, or provenance relations and affordances. A condition does not entail its transition, and a completed transition does not entail continued persistence. Completed, active, and nonactualized are mutually exclusive phases of one bounded transition at one reference time. Lexical nonactualization asserts a typed process that is not occurring; runtime `[2]/num` separately marks uncertainty about any selected phase claim.

**Runtime-state interpretation:**

- `[0]`: Denies that this source-ground establishment reached completion; it does not assert that the active or nonactualized sibling holds and does not reverse the transition.
- `[1]`: Affirms completion of this source-ground establishment without asserting persistence, success under an external criterion, cause, observation, or physical mechanism.
- `[2/num]`: Leaves completion of this source-ground establishment unresolved; runtime uncertainty does not change the lexical root into active or nonactualized phase.

**Lexical/runtime distinction:** Lexical third trit `0` permanently selects the completed phase of this ontological transition. Runtime `[2]/num` instead leaves the already-selected phase claim unresolved; it never changes process phase. Thus a nonactualized root affirmed with `[1]` confirms that the typed transition is not occurring, while a completed or active root carrying `[2]/num` remains lexically completed or active but uncertain as a token claim.
