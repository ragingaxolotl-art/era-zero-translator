# Reusing atomic conditions inside desire

This extends the candidate desire construction, not the canonical vocabulary.
The existing 324 roots, compound definitions and musical identities are unchanged.

Dependency review against `pages/data/index.json` and the existing description
frames in `pages/sentences.mjs` selects three existing predicates:

- WRN:001: a presently realized internal impairment condition, bearer role.
  Wanting to be damaged does not assert a damaging event or identify its agent.
- REP:001: a realized internal restoration condition, bearer role. Wanting to be
  restored does not assert an ongoing repair process, restorer or guaranteed result.
- MIN:201: available attentional capacity, bearer role. Wanting to be able to pay
  attention does not assert present attention or supply its target.

In “Nora wants to be restored,” the bearer is explicitly bound to this desire's
experiencer. In “Nora wants Eli to be restored,” Eli remains an independent
participant. Repeating the spelling Nora in the second construction does not
silently introduce identity. A nested finite desire binds an inner infinitive to
the nearest desire holder, not to the outer holder.

Desired conditions remain intensional: the sentence asserts desire, not the
condition's truth. Negation inside the content is distinct from negating desire.
Each predicate retains its independent runtime 0/1/2. Existing condition sounds
are embedded unchanged with the established content and subject-reference markers.
The independent MIDI inverse checks each predicate's bearer frame explicitly.

The new suite checks 54 distinct predicate/state/negation performances against
Ruby rendering and MIDI-only meaning recovery, plus explicit, deictic and nested
participants, past questions and invalid role rejection. It is an authored
regression suite, not evidence of general English coverage or listening validation.

## Follow-up discovered during review

LIF:011 and LIF:012 explicitly describe an “other” in the source dictionary.
The existing ordinary description routes deserve a viewpoint audit before
extending controlled “I want to be conscious/dormant” forms. This change does not
equate lexical dormancy with runtime 2 or silently erase the self/other distinction.
The subsequent [viewpoint review](desired_consciousness.md) confirms the existing
speaker-specific root selection and extends desire while preserving it.
Infinitival nested desire (“Nora wants Eli to want …”) remains unsupported;
explicit finite content (“Nora wants that Eli wants …”) is supported.
Desire still requires candidate opt-in; its vocabulary admission and broader
completion gates remain open.
