# Ordinary physical comparative forms

The selected senses of hotter, colder, heavier and lighter reuse the established two-participant comparison grammar. Hotter/colder compare thermodynamic temperature; heavier/lighter compare physical mass. Their participants, dimension, direction and strict inequality are explicit. No new root, compound, convention code or musical frame is required.

They are structural English routes, not aliases for hot/cold/heavy/light atoms or contextual norms. Something can be heavier than a feather without being heavy under the local standard. Not heavier permits equal mass, while lighter does not. Mass is not weight force or carrying effort, and thermodynamic temperature is not felt warmth, spiciness or emotional tone. Choosing another sense leaves the message unresolved.

The parser binds the complete comparison before composing supported negation, time, questions and proposition-level uncertainty. Runtime 0/1/2 is independent of those meanings. Source nouns and proper names retain the existing phonemic reference behavior. The selected physical reading does not establish those referents' real-world properties.

The shared inverse validates the existing comparison frame, both roles, permitted material root, register, runtime and enclosing scopes. Its subtree reader also supports framed groups containing comparisons, contextual physical descriptions and the existing simple named-state predicates. Ordered groups require the actual one-beat clause boundary. Embedded members are proposition contents, not independent assertions. Nested groups, other predicate profiles and external identity across repeated sound labels remain outside this grouped inverse.

No numerical difference, degree, superlative, comparative noun modifier, missing comparison participant, ellipsis or generalized calendar expression is silently supplied. The change is a bounded extension of normal sentence composition, not unrestricted English or microphone decoding. Tests and release evidence are recorded separately after execution. Approval is author semantic review under the user's project instruction, not a separate user review of every wording.
