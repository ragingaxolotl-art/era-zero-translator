# Explicit conventional meanings before sensory vocabulary

The inventory review in `perception_foundation_review.md` found no existing sensory
channel or sensory-access invariant. Combining generic information transfer,
representation and attention cannot select hearing rather than seeing. Adding an
English alias would hide that missing distinction.

The implemented `EZLEX-1` extension preserves all 324 roots and their sounds. It
allows an additional **explicit conventional meaning** on a compound, includes
that meaning in the content identity, and supplies a distinct ternary musical
frame around exactly that compound's payload. This is a lexical extension, not a
claim that the existing roots already entail the new meaning. The graph remains
true; a convention cannot turn information transfer into experience by silently
discarding the graph's requirements.

The fixed eight-trit code is a convention identifier. Its trits use the existing
0/1/2 pitch vocabulary and do not carry the occurrence ending. Opening and closing
frames preserve nested reference boundaries and participant role frames. Only the
external lexical head receives the compound runtime ending, while bound
participants retain their own endings. No existing entry receives a marker.

This route adds learned lexical distinctions without manufacturing a new LIF- or
STR-sized family. It is not semantically free: listeners and decoders need the
versioned convention dictionary. The explicit extra meaning and its sound must
remain inspectable. Codes must never be recycled after release. A renamed English
alias does not need a new code; a changed semantic commitment does.

The next vocabulary work follows dependencies:

1. Define sensory registration, sensory experience and successful perceptual
   access separately, including device versus conscious-experiencer boundaries.
2. Choose graphs whose component commitments remain true for each definition.
   Reject any convenient graph that silently requires attention, communication,
   representation change or consciousness where the intended sense does not.
3. Establish channel identities and the exact additional commitments they supply.
4. Build seeing and hearing from the relevant foundation and channel; only then
   build attentive looking/listening, recognition and sensory adjectives.
5. Validate ordinary sentences and contrasting music, including remembered versus
   currently perceived targets, hallucinated appearance versus actual access,
   and all three runtime endings.

At this stage the registry has **no approved conventional senses**. The tests use
explicitly artificial records to prove identity, scope and musical distinctions;
they do not certify the semantics of perception. The unchanged lexical inventory
remains 324 roots and 32 approved compounds.
