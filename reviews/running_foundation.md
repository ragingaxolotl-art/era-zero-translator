# Bodily running after locomotor activity

This author-reviewed extension specializes the existing bodily locomotor compound
(convention 20), preserving its actor and body-local physical commitments. New
convention 23 explicitly adds running gait; root adjacency alone does not supply it.
The conventional category uses repeated leg-support/recovery and bouncing or
compressive support as its mechanical characterization. It is not a universal
biomechanical classifier inferred from raw motion observations.

Grounded running must remain possible: van Bijlert et al. describe running without
an aerial phase and distinguish bouncing from vaulting dynamics in their bird
simulation study: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11423892/ . That paper
supports these contrasts; it does not validate this language definition across all
organisms or machines. In-place execution retains the bodily pattern without
asserting displacement. Walking, passive carriage, fast wheeled travel, liquid
flow and program execution do not receive the bodily-running sense.

This is an additive convention, with no new roots and no changed previous compound.
The first sentence route covers ongoing running with a named or deictic actor,
questions, negation, time and runtime 0/1/2. Destination and completed-journey
composition remain separate obligations; bare simple-present runs requires an
aspect decision before it can be treated as an episode or habit.

Validation must recover the gait convention, actor, scope and runtime from MIDI
without the source sentence, compare Ruby and browser rendering, reject a wrong
physical root, and distinguish swimming and generic movement. Author review and
synthetic MIDI checks do not establish listening or broad English coverage.
