# Consciousness, dormancy and the speaker's viewpoint

The ordinary sentence parser already selects LIF:001 for “I am conscious” and
LIF:002 for “I am dormant.” Its corresponding other roots are LIF:011 and
LIF:012. The concern recorded in the preceding atomic-condition review is
resolved by inspecting that existing selection, not by redefining these roots.

Candidate desire now reuses the same selection in infinitival propositions.
“I want to be conscious” retains LIF:001; “Nora wants to be conscious” retains
LIF:011. The content is not a quotation and does not shift the utterance's
speaker to Nora. Binding the omitted subject to the desire holder therefore does
not automatically make an other root into a self root.

“Nora wants me to be conscious” names the speaker explicitly and uses LIF:001
with a deictic bearer. The objective-case pronoun becomes the grammatical
subject of the embedded proposition; it is not a phonemic label spelled “me.”
An explicit repeated name remains an independent reference unless identity is
otherwise established. Finite nested desire preserves the same utterance-level
viewpoint while each controlled subject binds to its nearest desire holder.

Lexical dormancy and runtime 2 remain independent. LIF:002 and LIF:012 keep their
own distinct root sounds with runtime 0, 1 or 2, inside a desire that separately
has runtime 0, 1 or 2. A desired conscious condition is not asserted as actual,
and a desired dormant condition is not substituted with a conscious root in
runtime 2. Negation is its own scope.

The authored suite checks 72 distinct self/other, condition, state and negation
performances with Ruby/browser parity and independent MIDI recovery, plus
addressee, explicit-speaker, nested, question and outer-negation cases.
No root, compound, convention or runtime meaning changes. Desire remains an
opt-in candidate; indirect-discourse viewpoint shifts, broad pronoun resolution,
listening validation and full translator completion remain open.
